# Dashboard Storytelling and Advanced Visuals

## Overview

Effective dashboards do more than display data—they tell a story. Power BI provides advanced visuals that help users identify trends, compare performance, and make informed decisions quickly.

## KPI Cards

KPI Cards are used to highlight important business metrics.

Examples:
- Total Sales
- Profit
- Customers
- Orders

They provide a quick summary of business performance.

---

## Matrix Visual

The Matrix visual is an advanced version of a table.

Features:
- Hierarchical reporting
- Expand and collapse functionality
- Subtotals and grand totals

Example:
- Product Sales by Region

---

## Small Multiples

Small Multiples divide a visual into multiple smaller charts for easy comparison.

Example:
- Monthly Sales by Country
- Revenue by Product Category

They help identify patterns across groups.

---

## Scatter Plot

Scatter Plots are used to analyze relationships between variables.

Example:
- Sales vs Profit
- Revenue vs Cost

Useful for identifying trends, clusters, and outliers.

---

## Waterfall Chart

Waterfall Charts show how positive and negative values contribute to a final result.

Example:
- Profit Analysis
- Revenue Changes

They are commonly used in financial reporting.

---

## Tooltips

Tooltips provide additional information when hovering over a visual.

Benefits:
- Reduces dashboard clutter
- Provides detailed insights
- Improves user experience

---

## Dashboard Storytelling Principles

Good dashboards should:

- Highlight key KPIs first
- Use consistent colors and formatting
- Keep layouts clean and organized
- Minimize unnecessary visuals
- Focus on actionable insights

A dashboard should guide users from high-level metrics to detailed analysis.

---

## Key Learnings

- Creating KPI-focused reports
- Using Matrix and Small Multiples
- Understanding Scatter and Waterfall Charts
- Improving report usability with Tooltips
- Applying dashboard storytelling principles
- Designing professional and interactive Power BI reports
